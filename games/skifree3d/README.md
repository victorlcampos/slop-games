# ⛷️ SkiFree 3D

A 3D take on **SkiFree** (Chris Pirih, 1991) — the Windows 3.1 game where you
ski down the mountain until the Abominable Snowman turns up and eats you.

Runs **100% on the client**: plain Three.js, no backend, no CDN, no external
asset of any kind. All the content — terrain, textures, models, sound — is
generated by code at load time.

Plays in **English and Portuguese** — the flags are in the top-right corner of
the menu card, and the choice carries over from the catalog.

---

## How to run it

Run `npm run build` (at the slop-games root or here) and **double-click
`dist/index.html`**. That's it.

It is a single ~702 KB file with the game and Three.js embedded. It needs no
server, no internet, no install and no process running — it works over `file://`,
offline, copied onto a USB stick.

Requirements: a browser with WebGL2 (Chrome, Firefox, Safari 15+, Edge).

<details>
<summary>Working on the code</summary>

The source lives in `src/` as ES modules. After editing, regenerate the single
file:

```bash
npm run build        # → dist/index.html
```

The build is slop-games' own (`slopkit/build`): esbuild bundles `src/main.js`,
injects it into `template.html` and minifies. Three comes from `vendor/`, pointed
at by an alias — that is what took the final file from 1.6 MB down to ~700 KB,
since the previous homegrown bundler didn't minify.

</details>

## Controls

| Key | On the ground | In the air |
|---|---|---|
| `←` `→` | turn / carve | spin |
| `↑` | tuck — more speed | backflip |
| `↓` | snowplough brake | frontflip |
| `space` | jump | — |
| `C` | change camera (chase · retro · close) | |
| `P` / `Esc` | pause · `M` sound · `R` restart | |

On touch screens the bottom half becomes three zones: left, jump, right.

**A crooked landing puts you down.** Close the spin before you touch the snow —
if you are misaligned, the wipeout is certain. Clean tricks are worth points and
raise the style multiplier.

## The original's obstacles

Everything that got in the way in 1991 is here:

- 🌲 **Trees** — you can't jump them, only dodge. The woods close in at the sides and bound the piste.
- 🪨 **Rocks** — the low ones can be jumped; the big ones can't.
- 🎿 **Ramps** — a launch into the air, where the tricks happen.
- 🚡 **Chairlift** — the towers knock you down. The chairs pass overhead, with people in them.
- 🚩 **Slalom gates** — red flag on the left, blue on the right, as in the original.
- 🏂 **Other skiers and snowboarders** — the snowboarders come from behind and aim at you.
- 🐕 **The dog** — runs across the piste and stops to mark its territory.
- 🏚️ **Chalets, stumps, signs and bushes** scattered across the mountain.
- 👹 **The Yeti** — wakes at **2,000 m** and never tires. He speeds up as he chases.

### About the Yeti

In the 1991 game the Yeti always won — there was no escape. Here the rule is
nearly the same, with one concession to playability: open up **130 m** of a lead
and he gives up and sinks into the snow — but he comes back 12 seconds later,
faster, and every return is worse than the last. In other words: you can put it
off, never win.

## Modes

| Mode | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Free Ride** | The whole mountain, no rules. Yeti at 2,000 m. |
| **Slalom** | Gates every 42 m. Hitting one gives 150 points and raises the style; missing takes 120 and zeroes it. |
| **Forest Slalom** | Thick woods with a winding 34 m corridor. Yeti already at 1,600 m. |
| **Freestyle** | Ramps everywhere. The score comes from the air: spins, flips and hang time. |

Scoring: 1 point per metre × style multiplier (climbs every 120 m without a
crash, up to ×5; any wipeout zeroes it). Best scores live in `localStorage`,
separately per mode.

## How it was built

### Floating origin
The player never leaves the scene origin. The whole world lives in a
`worldGroup` shifted to `(0, SLOPE·z, −z)` every frame, so the skier is always at
`z = 0` and near `y = 0`. That keeps the matrices small and removes the `float32`
precision shimmer after a few kilometres of descent — with no coordinate
rebasing.

### Terrain
A grid of 7 × 10 chunks of 80 m, recycled as you descend. The height comes from a
single function (`groundHeight` in `config.js`) used by the mesh, the physics,
the AI and the object placement alike — mesh and collision never diverge.

The normals are computed on an extended grid with an extra ring of vertices,
which gives perfect continuity between neighbouring chunks (no visible seam, the
usual giveaway of chunked terrain). Rebuilds are queued and capped at 2 chunks
per frame, so there is no stutter.

### Snow
`MeshPhysicalMaterial` with a procedural normal map (grain + wind sastrugi,
generated on a canvas and tileable) sampled at two scales to kill the tile
repetition, `sheen` for the velvet of fresh powder and a thin `clearcoat` of ice
crust. Vertex colours darken the hollows by the laplacian of the height and
expose rock where it is steep. On top of that, a twinkle pass injected via
`onBeforeCompile`: crystals isolated by a 3D hash that only light up when their
random normal aligns with the halfway point between sun and camera.

### Lighting and atmosphere
- **Preetham sky** (three's `Sky`) with Rayleigh/Mie scattering — the same sky
  generates the **environment map** through `PMREMGenerator`, so the ambient
  light comes physically from the air.
- **Cascaded shadows (CSM)**, 4 levels out to 190 m: crisp next to the skier and
  still present in the distance, which a single shadow map of that range cannot
  deliver.
- **Aerial perspective**: three's fog was replaced (a patch on the global
  `ShaderChunk`s) with one that changes colour with the viewing direction —
  golden towards the sun, blue with your back to it. It is what ties the far
  terrain to the sky.
- **A low sun (12°), ahead** of whoever is descending. It is the only position
  where the disc fits in the frame — the camera looks downhill, so any high sun
  ends up off screen and leaves nothing for bloom or god rays. It also throws rim
  light and long shadows.

### Post-processing
`RenderPass → GTAO → god rays → bloom → OutputPass → lens`, with 4× MSAA on the
render target.

- **GTAO** (ground-truth ambient occlusion) to give volume to trunks, tracks and
  folds in the terrain.
- **Volumetric god rays** in screen space: march the pixel towards the sun,
  accumulating whatever is very bright. Since the solar disc is the only
  genuinely blown-out object, the trees covering it cut the rays out for free.
- **Lens**: radial blur that grows with speed, chromatic aberration, vignette,
  grain and colour grading with split toning (cool shadows, warm highlights).
- **Neutral tone mapping** (Khronos PBR Neutral) instead of ACES: at this
  exposure range ACES washed the sky's blue to white (measured chroma dropped
  from 43 to 17).

All the calibration — exposure, sky intensity, bloom and ray thresholds — was
measured by reading the pixels of the rendered frame, not eyeballed.

### Adaptive quality
GTAO costs ~35% of the frame. Instead of pinning a preset, the game measures the
first real seconds and turns off whatever is most expensive if the sum doesn't
close (GTAO → bloom and rays → pixel ratio).

### Objects
Everything instanced (`InstancedMesh` with a slot pool and a free list). The world
is divided into 25 m bands generated deterministically from the band index — the
mountain is always the same, and every band can be discarded and rebuilt without
storing state.

### Sound
Not one audio file: a pink-noise buffer generated once feeds biquad filters for
the wind and the ski friction (gain and frequency follow the speed), and
oscillators make up the jumps, impacts, gates and the Yeti's roar (a low sawtooth
with vibrato and a descending filter).

## Structure

```
dist/index.html       ← the game: single file, opens on a double click (generated)
template.html         interface, HUD and menus; the bundle goes in at /*__APP__*/
build.mjs             three lines calling slopkit's build
game.json             metadata that feeds the slop-games index
src/
  main.js             bootstrap and the wiring to the UI
  game.js             scene, light, post-processing, main loop
  config.js           constants + groundHeight (the terrain's source of truth)
  input.js            keyboard and touch
  audio.js            WebAudio synthesis
  hud.js              all the DOM
  i18n.js             the runtime dictionary (the static copy is inline in the template)
  lib/noise.js        Perlin, fbm and deterministic hashes
  world/
    sky.js            Preetham sky, mountain ranges, clouds, environment map
    terrain.js        recyclable chunks
    snowMaterial.js   the snow material + procedural normal map
    geometries.js     pines, rocks, ramps, chairlift, flags, chalets
    props.js          band-based population + colliders
    lift.js           towers, cables and chairs
  entities/
    skierModel.js     the articulated model (player and NPCs)
    player.js         physics, jumps, tricks, crashes
    npcs.js           skiers, snowboarders and the dog
    yeti.js           model and chase
  render/
    postfx.js         god rays and the lens pass
    atmosphere.js     aerial perspective (a patch on three's fog)
  fx/
    particles.js      snow spray and bursts
    snowfall.js       snowfall (animated in the vertex shader)
    trail.js          the ski trail
vendor/               Three.js r170 + addons (local copy, runs offline)
```
